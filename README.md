# Warrant

Kairos's laws-and-authority repository: where decisions are inspected, not where they are made.

**Status:** started 2026-11-17. Named by Zach in DEC-251 (relay-kit-private #147), which ratified the Convention (named Docket). Private for now.

## What it is for

- **Find incompatible decisions systematically,** instead of bit by bit as today.
- **Track each decision's history:** when it was made, by whom, on what basis, and how decisions link (supersedes, revokes, cites).
- **Inspect authority:** what each decision actually authorizes, who holds a grant, and where a lane acted past its quoted words.
- **Give challenges a structured path** to raise, answer and dispose of, in place of arguments in GitHub comments.
- **Later, if Zach grants it:** adjudicate smaller disagreements, so he doesn't have to decide everything.

## What it is not

- **Not the decision ledger.** `relay-kit-private/governance/` stays the authoritative record; Warrant reads it.
- **Not enforcement.** The Convention chose a read-only index and conflict report as the first artifact (synthesis §2 C–D). Enforcement and custody are separate decisions, for Zach.
- **Not a place to republish private owner words.** Reports cite a decision's id and digest; they don't quote private sources.

## First work

1. **The read-only challenge docket** (HO-RES-20261117-9AWM): each open challenge with its target and version, source, age, reply and disposition. 36 challenges are open as of 2026-11-17.
2. **The DEC compatibility shadow** (HO-RK-20261117-YDFR): references resolved to historical bytes and effective standing.
3. **A conflict report:** pairs of decisions whose scopes overlap and whose texts disagree.

## Clauses this repo carries (DEC-251)

> "An adopted decision remains citable as 'adopted, contested by CH-x', with its open challenge visible. A challenge alone changes no effective standing. Suspending another actor requires an existing scoped grant or a separate owner ruling naming scope and expiry."

Consequential amendments get a non-author read-back, which reports source authenticity, interpretation and scope separately.
